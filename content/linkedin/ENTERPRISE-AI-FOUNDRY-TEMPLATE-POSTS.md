## POST 4: Why 15 Days (Not 90) Matters

**Goal**: Challenge the "MVP speed" metric, reframe what matters

**Post**:

```
"How fast can you build an MVP?"

Wrong question.

For years, I measured modernization success by MVP velocity. 
How many days to build something?

But I've learned the real question is:

"How quickly can you determine if it's worth building?"

These are completely different.

Building something fast means nothing if you're building the wrong thing.

Here's what I've seen at federal agencies and enterprises:

You spend 90 days building an MVP.
You spend 3 months testing it.
You spend 2 months fixing bugs.
You present it to stakeholders for approval.
Stakeholders ask for changes (priorities shifted in 6 months).
You pivot and waste the previous 6 months of work.

Total: 8 months, millions spent, to learn "that wasn't what we needed."

Enterprise AI Foundry flips this:

15 days: Test assumptions
- Can we validate filings in real-time? 
- Can we search knowledge across systems?
- Can we score risk accurately?
Answer: Yes or no, without building full system.

30 days: Prove it works at scale
- Deploy to one system (case management, trade enforcement, knowledge platform)
- Measure accuracy, speed, business impact
- Decide: Expand or pivot?

If "yes": 60 more days to add 2-3 more systems.
If "no": You saved 6 months and millions.

The difference?

You validate before committing.

Success isn't "how fast we build." 
It's "how fast we know if it's worth building."

That changes everything.

Question: How much time and money is your org spending on projects that might not work?
```

- Build system → Test it → Audit it → Deploy it

Compliance happens AFTER you build.

Enterprise AI Foundry flips this.

Every component (Ask-AI adapter, Risk Scoring, Document Management, etc.)

- Audit trails on every decision
- Role-based access controls
- Data lineage tracking
- Control evidence generated at runtime
- Security scanning automated

Instead of: Build → Test → Audit (6 months)

You get: Build with compliance → Runtime evidence → Fast audit (2 weeks)

ATO (Authority to Operate) becomes telemetry-driven, not document-driven.

Why?

Because evidence is generated continuously, not assembled at the end.

We're not doing anything radical. Just shifting when compliance happens

Federal agencies and commercial enterprises with high compliance burden

Not because we're faster (though we are).

Because we reduce audit friction by making compliance visible continuously.
