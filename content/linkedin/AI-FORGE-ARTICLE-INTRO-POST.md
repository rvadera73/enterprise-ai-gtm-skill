DevSecOps, CI/CD, and Continuous ATO share one purpose, by their own definitions: replacing periodic, point-in-time practice with something continuous. Not speed. Continuity.

That standard is genuinely hard to hold by hand. A team commits to scanning every commit, and six months into real deadline pressure, quietly reverts to scanning before a release — not because anyone decided to cut corners, but because sustaining "continuous" manually, at real volume, is exhausting.

The open question isn't whether more automation helps. It's whether an engineering organization can actually hold the standard it already set for itself, without needing manual effort to grow at the same rate as the workload does.

A new piece looks at what that requires stage by stage, from how a requirement gets captured through to what happens once a release reaches production, including where the early evidence is solid and where it still needs validating against a real baseline rather than taken as a universal number.

Full piece linked in the comments.

Where does this actually break down inside a real organization — governance, tooling, headcount, something else entirely? Disagreement is as useful here as agreement.

Worth a share if it's relevant to anyone else wrestling with the gap between "we do continuous X" on paper and actually sustaining it in practice.
