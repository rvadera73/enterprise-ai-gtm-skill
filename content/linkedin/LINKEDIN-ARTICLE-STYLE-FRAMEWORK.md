# LinkedIn Article Style Framework (long-form, ~1000 words)

Derived 2026-08-19 from the author's own published LinkedIn Article, "TrueBid.AI:
Re-Architecting Proposal Development with Skills and MCP"
(linkedin.com/pulse/truebidai-re-architecting-proposal-development-skills-rahul-vadera-zuh7e).
Apply this by default to any future long-form LinkedIn Article (the "blog" content
type), for any product — distinct from `LINKEDIN-POST-STYLE-FRAMEWORK.md`, which
governs the short post that pairs with and links to the article. The two frameworks
coexist: this one governs voice and shape at article length; the post framework
still governs the short-form mechanics of what links to it.

## 1. Institutional voice, not personal narrative
Write from an organizational "we"/system voice, not a first-person anecdote. The
reference article never tells a personal story about one incident — it describes
what a system does and why, in an advisory, analytical register. Avoid opening
with "I've spent the last five months..." or an incident-first hook told as a
memory. If a real incident is genuinely the best evidence, cite it as a fact the
system produced ("a dispatch surfaced two genuine defects"), not as a story being
told to the reader.

## 2. Executive Summary opens with a paradox, in quotes
Open with a short, bolded Executive Summary paragraph structured as: the old
approach solved problem A, but created consequential problem B. Include one
quoted sentence inside it that states the paradox as crisply as possible — this
is the line a reader screenshots. Example shape: "The 'first wave' solved the
blank-page problem through X, but for Y, that has hit a ceiling — the new
bottleneck isn't A, it's B."

## 3. Four numbered sections, bold titles, ~150-200 words each
Structure the body as exactly four numbered sections, each with a short bold
title stating its claim (not a vague label): the old paradox in full, the
architecture/mechanism that resolves it, the concrete engine or protocol that
operationalizes it, and a forward-looking close. Keep each section tight —
150-200 words, not more — rather than one long undifferentiated essay.

## 4. One bulleted taxonomy, nowhere else
Use exactly one bulleted list in the whole piece, in the architecture section,
each item a bold role/component name followed by a colon and what it does. This
is where the piece proves the mechanism has real, distinct parts rather than
being one vague capability. Do not scatter bullets through other sections —
prose carries the argument everywhere else.

## 5. Declarative punches inside explanatory prose
Medium-length paragraphs (3-5 sentences) with at least one short, standalone
declarative sentence per section — a flat statement of fact or consequence, not
a hedge. "We have traded a writing problem for a validation crisis" is the
model: short, quotable, no qualifier.

## 6. Close by reframing the reader's role, not just the product
End on what changes for the people involved — from one posture to another
("Writers-in-Chief of AI noise" to "Strategic Architects of high-confidence
responses") — not a generic call-to-action or a repeated feature list. The close
should feel like a promotion, not a pitch.

## How to apply
Draft the article against all six before publishing. If a draft leans anecdotal,
casual ("look at the graphic above and you'll see..."), or listy in more than one
section, rewrite rather than patch. Word count target: ~1000-1400 for a full
article (the paired teaser/short post stays governed by its own, shorter
framework).

## Correction, 2026-08-19: rules 3 and 4 are a device, not a mandate
A later AI Forge draft (`AI-FORGE-DEVSECOPS-CATO-ARTICLE.md`), rewritten by the
author from scratch after several AI-drafted attempts leaned preachy and
scaffolded, used zero numbered sections and zero bulleted lists — pure flowing
prose — and was a stronger piece than any of the templated drafts. What actually
carried it: the *purpose* attributed to the underlying practices was verified
against primary sources rather than asserted (DevSecOps/CI-CD/cATO's own documented
purpose is continuity replacing point-in-time practice, not "empowering
engineers" — empowerment is downstream of that, not the founding claim); every
metric was hedged against the reader's own baseline ("should be viewed as
implementation results to validate against an organization's own baseline rather
than universal guarantees") instead of stated as a flat fact; and accountability
was stated plainly once, not repeated as a rhetorical device. Numbered sections
and one bulleted taxonomy remain a reasonable default scaffold when a draft is
running unfocused, but do not force them onto a draft that's already working as
prose — the four items in rules 1, 2, 5, and 6 (institutional voice, sourced
paradox, declarative punches, forward-looking close) are what actually matter;
3 and 4 are structural training wheels, not the goal.
