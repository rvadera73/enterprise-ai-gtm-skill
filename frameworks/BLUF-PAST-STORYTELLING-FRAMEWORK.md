# BLUF + PAST — the core storytelling framework for this skill

Use for GTM strategy, executive presentations, customer conversations, proposals,
and sales messaging. Cross-cutting — not specific to video or LinkedIn, though
`content/video/GTM-ASSET-PLAYBOOK.md`'s narrative-spine rules (BLUF as a fact/tension,
one concrete contrast, benefit as consequence) are one specific application of it.
That doc's rules and this framework should read as consistent, not competing — this
file is the general form; the video playbook's rules are the video-specific
instance. (Cross-reference note: `GTM-ASSET-PLAYBOOK.md`'s narrative spine section
should point back here once that doc's current PR lands — not done yet at the time
this file was added, to avoid a merge conflict with in-flight work.)

The objective: make every narrative clear, outcome-oriented, and persuasive, while
giving the audience enough context to understand and believe the conclusion.

## BLUF comes first

Open with the most important conclusion, recommendation, customer value, or decision
required. Do not make the audience wait through background to find the point. A
strong BLUF answers one question: **"What do I want the audience to know, believe,
or do?"**

## Then PAST builds the story

- **P — Past / Problem.** The relevant situation, customer pain, or market
  condition. Why it mattered.
- **A — Action.** What was done — the strategic choice made, the approach taken.
- **S — Shift / Solution.** What changed as a result. Measurable outcomes,
  differentiation, the new capability created.
- **T — Today / Takeaway.** Connect the story to now. Why the change matters
  today, and what should happen next.

Mental model, one line: **What → why it mattered → what we did → what changed →
why it matters now → what should happen next.**

## Adapt, don't apply mechanically

Depth and sequence should match the audience and the objective. An executive may
need a 30-second BLUF and a two-sentence PAST. A customer proposal may need the full
PAST arc with supporting evidence, metrics, and competitive differentiation. The
framework's job is to keep the conclusion up front and the story load-bearing — not
to force every piece of content through the same four labeled beats.

## Iterate against real outcomes

Continuously test and refine using customer feedback, sales outcomes, executive
reactions, and market research — the goal is not "a good story" in isolation, it's a
narrative that makes business value, urgency, differentiation, credibility, and the
desired action immediately clear to a specific real audience.

## Applying this to the AI-Assisted Software Engineering campaign

See [[ai-forge-gtm-campaign-mission-and-arc]] (memory) for the campaign this is
being applied to first. Concretely, per piece:

- **BLUF** is the single claim a stranger should understand cold — the existing
  BLUF-compression check already in `GTM-ASSET-PLAYBOOK.md` §13.4 (3 lines or fewer)
  is this framework's BLUF rule, independently arrived at from the Sandeep Swadia
  research; the two should be read as confirming each other, not duplicated content.
- **P (Past/Problem)** for this campaign is the real, general software-engineering
  pain (not DOL/ESD/GSS-specific) — e.g. review bottlenecks, compliance evidence
  collected after the fact instead of continuously, inconsistent delivery quality
  across teams.
- **A (Action)** is the real AI-DLC mechanism — agents as governed peer
  contributors, worktree-isolated parallel work, PR gates.
- **S (Shift/Solution)** is the measurable/observable change — e.g. compliance
  evidence generated continuously instead of retrofitted, consistent delivery
  pattern across parallel work — stated as consequence, not adjective, per the
  video playbook's existing rule.
- **T (Today/Takeaway)** closes on a question or an invitation to follow the
  practice develop, not a product CTA — no product exists publicly yet to call
  anyone to action on.
